# Application Process

> The 45-minute daily block. What actually happens between "I found a posting" and "submitted".
>
> Target: **10-12 minutes per application**, including the outreach check.

---

## The five steps

For every posting in the "apply today" bucket from
[the morning sweep](02-EARLY-APPLICATION-SYSTEM.md):

### 1. Outreach check — 2 min

**Is this a target company?**

- **Yes, and someone has been contacted** → proceed
- **Yes, and nobody has** → find one person, send the message *now*, then proceed. You do not
  wait for a reply ([Rule 1](../00_Start_Here/THE-RULES.md))
- **Yes, and you genuinely cannot find a single human** → log the exception in `outreach.csv`
  with reason `no-contact-found`, then proceed
- **No, it is a Tier C or volume application** → exempt, proceed

**Also check:** do you already know someone there? Second-degree connections count. A LinkedIn
search on the company before applying takes 60 seconds and occasionally changes everything.

### 2. Analyse the JD — 3 min

Extract:

- **The real must-haves.** Usually 5-8 requirements under the boilerplate
- **The exact vocabulary** they use for the skills you have
- **Disqualifiers** — work authorization, location, years of experience stated as hard minimums,
  a clearance you do not have

**On the "years of experience" line:** apply anyway if you are within a couple of years and match
the substance. Those numbers are usually aspirational, and the rest of the posting tells you what
they actually need.

**Ghost check:** posted 3+ months? Reappearing unchanged every 30 days? Every technology in the
industry on one req? Downrank, but still apply if fit is strong — just do not do outreach or
custom tailoring work for it.

### 3. Tailor — 10 min, hard cap

From your master resume. Reorder, swap projects, mirror their vocabulary in your top third.
Details in [the resume playbook](08-RESUME-AND-ATS.md).

**The cap is the point.** Going over means the posting is aging while you polish. A dream role
with a referral in hand is the one exception worth the extra half hour.

### 4. Submit — 5 min

- Fill the form properly. Do not abandon a long application form halfway — that is time already
  spent, thrown away
- **The "why do you want to work here" box:** two or three real sentences, not a paragraph and
  not "I am passionate about your mission". Name something specific
- **Salary expectation field:** a researched range, never a single number. If it forces one, use
  the top of your researched range
- **"How did you hear about us":** if someone referred you, name them. This is often a routed
  field that changes who reads your application
- Attach the version you tailored, not the one from last Tuesday

### 5. Log it — 2 min

`04_Trackers/applications.csv`. Including **hours since posting** — this is the number that lets
you check later whether the 24-hour rule is actually being followed, and whether it is working
for you.

## Depth versus volume

You need both, in a deliberate ratio.

| | Targeted | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| **Applies to** | Tier A and B, referral in hand, strong fit | Tier C, adjacent roles, anything decent |
| **Time each** | 30-60 min | 8-12 min |
| **Outreach first?** | Always | Not required |
| **Cover letter?** | Sometimes | No |
| **Per week** | 3-5 | 10-20 |

**Start at roughly 70% volume / 30% targeted, then let your own data move it.** After six weeks
you will know which one is producing screens for you, and that is a better guide than any general
advice. Review it at the monthly retro.

---

## The application is not the end

Two things happen after submit that most people skip:

**Same day:** if you have a contact at the company, tell them you applied.

> "Just applied for the [role] posting — if you have any advice on the process or know who is
> running the search, I would appreciate it."

This is when a contact converts into a referral. Not before, not later.

**One week later, no response:** find the recruiter or hiring manager on LinkedIn and send a
short note.

> "I applied for [role] last week and am very interested — happy to share anything else that
> would be useful."

Low cost, occasionally decisive, and it pulls your application back to the top of a pile.

---

## Re-applying

**Re-apply to the same company.** It does not blacklist you. Applying to three roles at a company
over six months reads as sustained interest, not desperation, and different roles are read by
different people.

Wait for a genuinely different posting, and if you got feedback last time, act on it visibly.

## Tracking what matters

Log enough to answer these at the monthly retro:

| Question | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Applications per week | Are you actually working the process |
| % within 24h of posting | Whether the 5x lever is being pulled |
| Application → screen rate | Whether the resume passes |
| Rate for referred vs cold applications | Whether outreach-first is paying off — usually the most striking number in the whole tracker |
| Rate by source | Which job boards deserve your sweep time |
| Time per application | Whether tailoring is creeping past its cap |

**Benchmark:** a cold application → screen rate around 2-5% is normal and is not a verdict on
you. Referred applications routinely run several times that. When you see those two numbers side
by side in your own data, [Rule 1](../00_Start_Here/THE-RULES.md) stops feeling like an
imposition.

## Rejections

- **Log them.** Date, stage, reason if given, in `logs/rejections.md`
- **Look for the pattern, not the instance.** Ten rejections at the resume stage is a resume
  problem. Ten at the phone screen is a positioning or communication problem. Ten at final round
  is close, painful, and mostly luck. Each has a different fix, and only the pattern tells you
  which one you have
- **Most rejections carry no information.** No feedback, no reason, often no message at all.
  Do not construct a story out of silence
- **Ask once, politely,** for feedback. Roughly one in ten replies, and that one is valuable
- **Stay in touch with anyone you met.** A final-round rejection often means "we hired someone
  else this time", and those people are contacts now. Some of the best outcomes in a search come
  from a company that rejected you six months earlier
