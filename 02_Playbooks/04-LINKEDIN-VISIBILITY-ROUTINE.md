# LinkedIn Visibility Routine

> The goal is not to go viral. The goal is that when someone at a target company sees your name
> on an application, it is **not the first time**.
>
> This is a months-long compounding play. It will feel pointless for six weeks. Do it anyway —
> the payoff arrives all at once, usually as a message from someone you never contacted.

---

## Why visibility works

Hiring is a trust problem under time pressure. A hiring manager with 400 applications is looking
for a reason to shortlist someone, and *recognition* is one of the strongest reasons available —
not because it proves competence, but because it converts you from a row in a spreadsheet into a
person.

Repetition builds recognition. Somebody who has seen your name on three thoughtful comments and a
post about a project you shipped is not evaluating a stranger.

The second-order effect matters just as much: **recruiters search LinkedIn.** A profile with
current keywords, recent activity, and a clear headline surfaces in searches you never knew were
happening. A dormant profile does not surface at all. A meaningful share of good roles arrive as
an inbound message rather than as a response to something you applied to — but only for people
who are visible.

**Timeline expectation:** weeks 1-6, nothing. Weeks 6-12, engagement starts. Months 3-6, inbound
messages begin. This is slow, which is exactly why starting it in week one matters — you cannot
compress it later when you need it.

## The profile, fixed once

Do this in one 90-minute sitting, then leave it alone for three months.

| Element | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Photo** | A clear one where you look approachable. Phone camera and a plain wall is fine. Not a party crop |
| **Banner** | Anything intentional. A default gradient reads as an abandoned profile |
| **Headline** | Not "Student". Say what you do plus what you are moving toward: *"CS grad · computer vision and ML pipelines · building [thing]"*. This is the most-read line on your profile and it is search-indexed |
| **About** | 3-4 short paragraphs. What you work on, one concrete achievement with a number, what you are looking for, how to reach you. Front-load keywords — the preview cuts off fast |
| **Experience** | Same bullets as your resume, same keywords. Recruiters search on these terms |
| **Skills** | Fill all available slots with terms that actually appear in your target JDs. This is a literal search index |
| **Featured** | Pin 2-3 things: a project, a repo, a write-up. It converts profile views into evidence |
| **Open to work** | Recruiters-only visibility is the low-risk setting. The public green banner is a personal call — it increases inbound and it is visible to your current employer |

**Keyword rule:** open five target JDs, list every recurring term, and make sure the ones you can
honestly claim appear in your headline, About, and Skills. That is the entire optimization.

## The weekly routine

**About 45 minutes a week, total.** Split across two blocks.

### Wednesday — the post (30 min)

**One post per week, minimum** ([Rule 8](../00_Start_Here/THE-RULES.md)).

It does not have to be good. It has to exist. The person posting a mediocre weekly update for six
months beats the person waiting to have something impressive to say — because the second person
posts nothing, ever.

**What to post** — full formats in
[../03_Templates/linkedin-post-formats.md](../03_Templates/linkedin-post-formats.md):

| Type | Example | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Project update** | What you built, one problem you hit, how you solved it. Screenshot | Medium |
| **Learning in public** | "Spent this week on X. Three things that surprised me:" | Low |
| **Certification / milestone** | Completed a course, passed a cert, shipped a repo | Very low |
| **A take** | An opinion on something in your field, with reasoning | Low |
| **Something you read** | An article plus 3 lines on why it changed your thinking. Tag the author | Very low |
| **Event recap** | Attended a meetup, what stood out. Tag speakers and people you met | Very low |

**What not to post:** "Looking for opportunities, please like and share" as your whole content
strategy. Once at a milestone is fine. Weekly, it is the only thing anyone knows about you.

**Format rules that actually affect reach:**

- Hook in the first line — everything after ~2 lines is behind "see more"
- Short paragraphs, whitespace, no walls of text
- 3-5 relevant hashtags, at the end
- Native content only. **Links in the post body suppress reach** — put the link in the first comment
- Text and images outperform link previews
- Reply to every comment. Comments in the first hour drive distribution more than likes
- Tuesday-Thursday morning is the standard best window

### Friday — engagement (15 min)

This is the part that actually generates conversations, and it is the part people skip because it
produces nothing visible.

1. **Comment on 3-5 posts** from your target companies or the people you have contacted
2. Substantive comments only. A real question, a relevant experience, a respectful disagreement.
   "Great post!" is worse than silence — it is visible effort with zero signal
3. **Like a few** — low value, near-zero cost
4. **Congratulate** anyone in your network on a new role or milestone. This is a real, warm,
   welcome contact that costs nothing

**Why comments beat posts for a job search:** your post reaches your own network, which is mostly
people who cannot hire you. Your comment on a target company employee's post reaches **their**
network — the people you are trying to be visible to. And the author gets a notification with
your name on it.

Ten thoughtful comments on the right posts beat one good post. Do both.

## Following your targets

For every Tier A and B company on your list:

- **Follow the company page** — you see their announcements, and their postings surface earlier
- **Follow (not connect with) the engineers** whose work is relevant. Following is one-way and
  requires nothing from them, and their posts start appearing in your feed, which gives you
  material to comment on
- **Turn on notifications** for 3-5 people whose posts you actually want to engage with
- **Connect properly** — with a note — when you have engaged a few times. That request lands
  warm instead of cold

This is the sequence that turns cold outreach into warm outreach: **follow → engage → connect
with a note → conversation.** Two to four weeks, mostly passive.

---

## What to do when a post does nothing

Most will do nothing. Three likes from people you already know is the normal outcome for months.

Post anyway. The audience for this is not the like count — it is the one recruiter who checks
your profile in November, sees eleven months of consistent activity, and reads that as someone
serious about their field.

---

## Crew angle

The single easiest lift a crew provides:

- **Engage with each other's posts within the first hour.** Early comments drive distribution.
  Two people commenting on each other's posts genuinely moves reach
- **Comment substantively, not "nice work"** — the algorithm and the humans both weight it
- **Trade post ideas at the check-in.** "I have nothing to post" is almost always false; your
  crew partner can usually name three things you did that week worth posting
- **Review each other's profiles once a quarter.** You cannot see your own headline objectively
  after the fifth read

Add LinkedIn posts and comments to the weekly scoreboard. It is the most skippable item in this
repo, which is precisely why it needs a number attached.
