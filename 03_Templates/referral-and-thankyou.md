# Referral Asks and Thank-You Messages

> A referral is the highest-value thing a contact can give you — and it costs them their
> reputation, not just five minutes. Ask for it well, and make it as close to zero work as
> possible.

---

## When to ask for a referral

| Situation | Ask? |
|---|---|
| Stranger, first message | **No.** This is the number one way outreach fails |
| After one good coffee chat | Yes, gently, with an explicit exit |
| After they offered | Obviously — and make it easy for them |
| Someone you have talked to a few times | Yes, directly |
| A friend or former colleague | Yes, just ask |
| A recruiter | Not applicable — they route you, they do not refer you |

**The rule: a referral requires that they can say something about you.** If they cannot, you are
asking them to spend credibility on a stranger, and most people will politely decline. One real
conversation is usually enough to clear that bar.

---

## Asking after a good conversation

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks again for the chat last week — the point about [specific thing they said]
has stuck with me, and it made me more interested in the team rather than less.

I noticed [company] has a [role] posting open ([link]). If you think it would be
appropriate, I'd really value a referral — but I completely understand if you'd
rather not for someone you've only spoken to once, and it wouldn't change my
appreciation for the conversation at all.

Either way, I'll be applying and I'll let you know how it goes.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

**Why the exit clause matters.** "No pressure, I understand if not" gives them a graceful way to
decline, which makes them *more* likely to say yes. Cornering someone into a referral gets you a
lukewarm one, which is worse than none.

## When they say yes — the packet

Send this within an hour. **Make the referral copy-pasteable.**

```
Thank you, [Name] — really appreciate it. Making it as easy as possible:

Role: [exact title] — [req number if there is one]
Link: [url]
Resume: attached

Three lines if you need something to say:
• [Achievement with a number, matching their top requirement]
• [Relevant experience, matching their second requirement]
• [Why this team specifically — one sentence, specific to them]

Happy to change any of this if you'd rather put it differently. And if you'd
prefer to just submit my name in the internal tool, that works too — let me
know if you need anything else from me.

Thanks again,
[Your name]
```

**Why this works:** most internal referral tools ask the referrer to write a short justification.
If you have written it for them, referring you takes ninety seconds instead of twenty minutes.
People who genuinely meant to refer you frequently do not, purely because it became a task.

---

## Asking someone you actually know

Skip the ceremony.

```
Hey [Name] — [company] has a [role] opening ([link]) and it's a genuinely good
fit for what I've been doing. Any chance you'd be up for referring me? Happy to
send anything that makes it easy.

No worries at all if it's awkward for any reason.
```

---

## Asking for an introduction (not a referral)

Lower cost to them, and very often more useful to you. This is the ask you should be making
constantly.

```
Hi [Name],

Quick ask — you mentioned [person/team] when we spoke. Would you be comfortable
introducing me? I'd like to hear how they approach [specific thing].

If it helps, here's something you could forward:

  "[Your name] is [one-line background]. They're looking at [role] work and
  were interested in how your team handles [thing]. I thought you two should
  talk."

No problem at all if you'd rather not.
```

**Always include the forwardable blurb.** It turns "write an introduction" into "hit forward".

## Thank-you messages

### After a coffee chat — within 24 hours

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for taking the time today — I really enjoyed it. The point about [very
specific thing they said] was genuinely useful; I hadn't thought about it that
way, and it's changed how I'm approaching [thing].

I'll follow up with [person they suggested] this week. Good luck with [the thing
they mentioned being busy with] — I'll keep an eye on what your team ships.

Thanks again,
[Your name]
```

**The specific detail is the whole message.** A generic thank-you is forgettable; a thank-you
that proves you were listening makes you memorable, and it is the reason they reply to you in
November.

### After an interview — within 24 hours, to each interviewer

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the conversation today. I enjoyed the discussion about [specific
technical thing] — I've been thinking about [the follow-on thought] since.

The role sounds like a strong fit for what I've been building toward, and I'd be
glad to be part of what your team is working on. Happy to provide anything else
that would be useful.

Best,
[Your name]
```

If you fumbled something in the interview, this is the place for one short correction — one
sentence, no apology, then move on.

### After they refer you

```
[Name] — thank you, genuinely. I know a referral isn't a small thing to put your
name to. I'll let you know how it goes either way.
```

### After you land the job — close the loop

**Send this to everyone you spoke to during the search.** Almost nobody does it, and it is the
message that converts a job-search contact into a lasting professional relationship.

```
Hi [Name],

I wanted to let you know I've accepted a role at [company] as a [title],
starting [month].

Your [advice / conversation / introduction] back in [month] genuinely helped —
[specific thing that mattered]. Thank you for taking the time.

If I can ever return the favour, please ask. Would be glad to stay in touch.

Best,
[Your name]
```

### After a rejection, to people who helped

```
Hi [Name],

Wanted to close the loop — I didn't get the [role] at [company]. Made it to
[stage], which I'll take as progress.

Thank you for [what they did]. Still very much looking, and I'll keep you posted.

Best,
[Your name]
```

Gracious rejection follow-ups are rare and are remembered. People who watch you handle a "no"
well will think of you when the next thing opens.
