# Cold Email Templates

> 150-200 words. Three paragraphs. **Resume attached, always.**
> Send Tuesday or Thursday, 10:00-11:00 AM their time, at a random minute.

---

## Subject lines

Specific, low-key, no hype. The subject decides whether the email is opened.

- `[University] grad — quick question about [team] at [company]`
- `Question about your work on [specific thing]`
- `Fellow [city] [role] — 15 minutes?`
- `[Mutual contact] suggested I reach out`  ← by far the strongest, when true
- `Following [company]'s [Series X] — quick question`

**Avoid:** "Opportunity", "Networking request", "Seeking guidance", anything with an exclamation
mark, and anything that reads like a newsletter.

---

## Template A — Alumni / shared background

The highest-response version. Use it whenever the shared thing is real.

```
Subject: Dalhousie grad — quick question about the perception team at Nuro

Hi Priya,

Always good to see another Dalhousie grad working in computer vision. I'm
finishing my MSc there now, and your name came up while I was looking at where
people from the program end up.

I've spent the last year building a vision pipeline for livestock monitoring —
multi-camera tracking on live video, running on edge hardware, currently
deployed on a working farm. I'm moving toward perception and ML engineering
roles, and the work your team is doing on real-time detection is very close to
what I want to be doing next.

If you have fifteen minutes in the next few weeks, I'd really value hearing
about your experience there and how you found the transition from school. I'm
usually free Tuesday and Thursday mornings, but happy to work around your
schedule.

Thanks for your time,
Sam Rivera
linkedin.com/in/samrivera · github.com/samrivera
[resume attached]
```

## Template B — Their specific work

For someone who published, spoke, or shipped something.

```
Subject: Question about your talk on distributed tracing

Hi [Name],

I watched your [conference] talk on [topic] last week — the part about
[specific detail] answered something I'd been stuck on in my own project.

I'm [one-line background] and I've been working on [your thing], where I ran
into [the related problem]. I ended up [what you did], which is why your
approach caught my attention. I'm looking at [role] roles now, and the kind of
problems [company] works on are exactly the ones I want to be working on.

Would you have fifteen minutes in the next couple of weeks for a quick chat?
I'd like to hear how the team approaches [thing] and what your path there
looked like. Happy to work around whatever time suits you.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn] · [GitHub]
[resume attached]
```

## Template C — Warm introduction

When someone referred you. Get the referrer's name into the first sentence and the subject line.

```
Subject: [Referrer] suggested I reach out

Hi [Name],

[Referrer] mentioned you'd be a good person to talk to about [topic] — we spoke
last week about [context] and your name came up as someone doing [specific work].

I'm [one-line background], most recently [achievement with a number]. I'm
focused on [area] and looking at [role] positions. [Referrer] thought the work
your team does on [thing] would line up well with where I'm trying to go.

Would you have fifteen minutes in the next few weeks? I'd value your perspective
on [specific question]. Happy to fit around your schedule.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn]
[resume attached]
```

## Template D — The funding play

Short window, low competition. Move within a week or two of the announcement.

```
Subject: Following [company]'s Series B — quick question

Hi [Name],

Congratulations on the Series B — I've been following [company] since [when/why]
and it's good to see [specific thing] getting backed.

I work on [your specialization]. A round like this usually goes toward [the
specific problem the funding is for], and that's the exact area I've been
building in: [one concrete achievement with a number]. I'd guess the next twelve
months are about [what scaling looks like for them].

Are you hiring on that side — people who can work on those specific problems?
Either way I'd value fifteen minutes to hear where the team is heading.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn] · [GitHub]
[resume attached]
```

**What makes this land:** you are talking about *their* problem at *their* level. It reads as a
peer who understands the business, not a candidate asking for consideration.

## Template E — Hiring manager, role is open

More direct, because there is a live req and they know it.

```
Subject: [Role] posting — [your specialization] background

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [role] position on [date] and wanted to reach out directly,
since I understand you're running that search.

Short version: [one sentence on your background]. The posting asks for
[requirement] — I [specific evidence, with a number]. It also mentions
[requirement 2], which is what I spent [timeframe] on at [context].

I'd welcome the chance to talk about the role, and I'm happy to share anything
that would be useful for the process.

Thanks for your time,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn]
[resume attached]
```

## Rules

**Structure — every paragraph has one job:**

| Para | Job |
|---|---|
| **1** | **The connection.** Shared school, shared field, a mutual contact, their work. Never open with "I am writing to inquire about" |
| **2** | **You, then them.** Two sentences on your background with one concrete number. Then what *they* are doing and why it interests you. That order |
| **3** | **The ask, with availability offered.** Fifteen minutes, specific windows, "happy to work around your schedule." Removes a scheduling round-trip |

**Do**

- Attach the resume every time
- Offer your availability in the message
- Proper signature with LinkedIn
- One clear ask
- Quantify one achievement in paragraph 2

**Do not**

- Exceed 200 words. Long emails do not get read, they get archived
- Ask for a referral in a first email
- Attach a cover letter
- Use "I hope this email finds you well"
- Apologise for emailing
- Send the same email to five people at one company
