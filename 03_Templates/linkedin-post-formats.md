# LinkedIn Post Formats

> One post a week, minimum ([Rule 8](../00_Start_Here/THE-RULES.md)). It does not have to be
> good. It has to exist.
>
> Pick a format, fill it in, post it. Twenty minutes maximum — a post you spend two hours on and
> then do not publish is worth less than a mediocre one that goes up.

---

## Universal format rules

- **Hook in line one.** Everything past ~2 lines is hidden behind "see more". The first line
  decides whether anyone reads the rest
- **Short paragraphs.** One to two sentences each, with blank lines between. Walls of text get
  scrolled past
- **No links in the body** — they suppress reach. Put the link in the first comment and say
  "link in comments"
- **3-5 hashtags at the end**, relevant not generic
- **Reply to every comment.** Comments in the first hour drive distribution more than likes do
- **Tuesday-Thursday morning** is the standard best window
- **Native content wins** — text, images, and carousels beat link previews

## Format 1 — Learning in public (lowest effort, always available)

```
Spent this week learning [thing].

Three things that surprised me:

1. [Surprise] — I'd assumed [assumption], but actually [reality].

2. [Surprise] — this one cost me [time/effort] before it clicked.

3. [Surprise] — still not sure I fully understand this one.

Next up: [what you're doing next].

#[topic] #[field] #learninginpublic
```

Works every single week, because you always learned something. This is the default.

---

## Format 2 — Project update

```
[One-line hook about what you built or shipped.]

The problem: [2 sentences.]

What I built: [2-3 sentences. Name the stack — those are your keywords.]

The part that was harder than expected: [the honest bit. This is what makes the
post worth reading — anyone can list a stack.]

What I'd do differently: [1-2 sentences.]

Repo in the comments.

#[stack] #[field]
```

Attach a screenshot, a diagram, or a short screen recording. Visual posts substantially
outperform text-only.

---

## Format 3 — Milestone or certification (very low effort)

```
Just finished [certification / course / milestone].

Honest take: [was it worth it? what did it actually teach you? what did it not?]

The most useful part was [specific thing], which I'm already applying to
[project].

If anyone's considering it, happy to answer questions.

#[topic] #[field]
```

The honest evaluation is what makes this a post rather than a humblebrag. "Was it worth it" is a
question people genuinely want answered.

---

## Format 4 — A take

```
[Opinion, stated plainly, as the first line.]

Here's what I keep running into:

[2-3 sentences of the concrete experience behind the opinion.]

I think the reason is [your reasoning].

Curious whether other people are seeing the same thing — or whether I'm wrong
about this.

#[field]
```

Ending with a genuine question drives comments, and comments drive reach. Do not manufacture a
hot take; a mild opinion you actually hold outperforms a spicy one you do not.

---

## Format 5 — Something you read

```
Read [article/paper] this week — [one line on why it stuck].

The part that changed how I think about it:

"[short quote or paraphrase]"

I'd been assuming [your prior]. This makes me think [updated view].

Worth a read if you work on [area]. Link in comments.

#[topic]
```

**Tag the author** if they are on LinkedIn. They often reply, which puts your post in front of
their whole network — this is one of the highest-leverage low-effort moves available.

---

## Format 6 — Event recap

```
Spent [day] at [event].

Three things I took away:

1. [Takeaway from a specific talk — name the speaker.]
2. [Takeaway.]
3. [Takeaway.]

Best conversation of the day was with [person, tagged] about [topic].

Thanks to [organisers, tagged] for putting it together.

#[event] #[field]
```

**Tag people.** It is the single most effective reach mechanism on the platform, and in this
context it doubles as a follow-up to everyone you met.

---

## Format 7 — Asking a question

```
Question for people working in [field]:

[Specific, genuinely answerable question. Not "any advice?"]

Context: [2 sentences on why you're asking.]

Would value any perspective.

#[field]
```

Low effort, generates real comments, and often starts conversations with exactly the people you
want to be talking to.

---

## Format 8 — The job search post (use sparingly)

Once, at a milestone. Not weekly.

```
[Milestone: graduating / finishing a contract / making a move.]

What I'm looking for: [role], [location or remote], [field].

What I bring: [2-3 concrete things with numbers.]

What I'm working on right now: [current project.]

If you know of anything, or just want to talk about [field], my inbox is open.

#opentowork #[field]
```

**Why sparingly:** a feed of nothing but "please hire me" tells people nothing about your work.
The other seven formats are what make this one land, because by the time you post it people
already know what you do.

## Comment templates

Comments matter more than posts for a job search — your post reaches your network, your comment
on a target-company employee's post reaches *theirs*.

**Add an experience:**
> We hit this exact problem building [thing]. What worked for us was [approach], though
> [caveat]. Curious whether you found the same.

**Ask a real question:**
> Interesting — how did you handle [specific edge case]? That's the part I've never found a
> clean answer for.

**Respectful disagreement (high visibility, use carefully):**
> Interesting take. I'd push back slightly on [point] — in my experience [counter-example].
> Though that might be specific to [context].

**Never:** "Great post!", "Thanks for sharing", "This 👏 is 👏 so 👏 true". Visible effort with
zero signal is worse than not commenting.

## Weekly rotation

Never wonder what to post:

| Week | Format |
|---|---|
| 1 | Project update |
| 2 | Learning in public |
| 3 | Something you read, or a take |
| 4 | Milestone, or event recap |

Repeat. Four formats, one month, no decisions required.
