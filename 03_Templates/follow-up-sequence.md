# Follow-Up Sequences

> **Most replies come from a follow-up, not from the first message.**
>
> People are busy, your message landed in a bad hour, they meant to reply and forgot. None of
> that is rejection. Two follow-ups, roughly a week apart, then stop
> ([Rule 7](../00_Start_Here/THE-RULES.md)).

---

## The cadence

| Message | Day | Length | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 0 | Full | |
| **Follow-up 1** | +7 | Short | Same thread. Add one new thing |
| **Follow-up 2** | +14 | Shorter | Give them an easy exit |
| — | +21 | — | Mark `cold`. Stop |

Always reply **in the same email thread** or the same LinkedIn conversation. A new thread loses
the context and reads as a fresh cold contact.

---

## Follow-up 1 — add something

The rule: never send a bare "just checking in". Add one new thing so the message has a reason to
exist.

**Version A — an article or development**

```
Hi [Name],

Following up on the below — no rush at all.

Since I wrote, I came across [article/announcement/development] on [topic],
which made me think of what your team is working on with [thing]. Curious
whether that's something you're seeing too.

Still very interested in a quick chat if you have fifteen minutes in the coming
weeks.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

**Version B — a company development**

```
Hi [Name],

Circling back on this — I saw [company] announced [thing] last week, which
makes me more curious about what the [team] side of that looks like.

Still keen on a short chat if you have time. Happy to work around your schedule.

Best,
[Your name]
```

**Version C — a specific question (highest reply rate)**

A question is much easier to answer than a meeting request. Sometimes the answer becomes the
conversation.

```
Hi [Name],

Following up on my note below. Rather than take up your time with a call —
would you mind if I asked one thing?

[One specific, genuinely answerable question about their work, their team, or
their path. Not "any advice?"]

Any thoughts appreciated, and no worries if now isn't a good time.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

**LinkedIn version (short)**

```
Hi [Name] — following up on my note. Saw [company] shipped [thing] recently,
which made me more curious about the team. Still would value a quick chat if you
have fifteen minutes. No worries either way.
```

## Follow-up 2 — the graceful exit

Shorter. Give them a clean way to say no, which paradoxically increases the reply rate — it
removes the guilt that stops people responding to something they have already left too long.

```
Hi [Name],

Last note from me on this — I know inboxes get away from all of us.

Still very interested in [company] and the work your team does. If a quick chat
is possible at any point I'd value it, and if not, no problem at all — I'll keep
following what you're building.

Thanks for your time either way,
[Your name]
```

**LinkedIn version**

```
Hi [Name] — last note from me, I don't want to clutter your inbox. Still would
value a chat if you're ever free, and if not that's completely fine. Either way,
good luck with [specific thing].
```

Then mark them `cold` in `04_Trackers/outreach.csv` and move on.

## Application follow-ups

Different sequence. This is about a live application, not a relationship.

**One week after applying, no response — to the recruiter or hiring manager:**

```
Hi [Name],

I applied for the [role] position on [date] and wanted to reach out directly —
I'm very interested in the role and in what [team] is working on.

Short version of my background: [one sentence with a concrete number]. Happy to
share anything else that would be useful for the process.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

**After an interview, when the stated timeline has passed by a few days:**

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks again for the conversation on [date] — I enjoyed hearing about [specific
thing].

You'd mentioned you expected to have an update around [timeframe], so I wanted
to check in. I'm still very interested, and happy to provide anything else that
would help.

Best,
[Your name]
```

**When you have another offer** — this genuinely accelerates processes:

```
Hi [Name],

I wanted to give you a heads-up: I've received an offer from another company
with a decision deadline of [date].

[Company] is my strong preference and I didn't want to make a decision without
checking where things stand on your side. Is there any possibility of moving the
process forward before then?

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

Only send this if it is true.

## Reactivating a cold contact

Three or more months later, with an actual reason. Not "checking in".

```
Hi [Name],

It's been a while — I reached out back in [month] about [company]. Since then
I've [what changed: shipped something, finished a degree, started a role,
learned the thing you discussed].

I saw [company] [recent development] and thought of our exchange. If you're ever
free for a quick chat I'd still value it.

Congratulations on [their promotion / their team's launch], by the way.

Best,
[Your name]
```

A `cold` contact is dormant, not dead. Six months of occasional comments on their posts turns a
non-reply into a warm contact — and their situation may have changed completely.

## Follow-up rules

**Do**

- Reply in the same thread
- Add something new every time
- Keep each one shorter than the last
- Give an easy exit in the second one
- Track the send dates in `outreach.csv` so this runs mechanically instead of from memory

**Do not**

- Send "just bumping this to the top of your inbox"
- Send more than two
- Express disappointment, or comment on their not replying
- Follow up after 2 days. A week is the floor
- Follow up on multiple channels at once — email *and* LinkedIn *and* Twitter in one week reads
  as pressure
