# LinkedIn Connection Notes

> ~300 character limit. That constraint is a gift — it stops you writing a cover letter.
>
> **Change at least three things per send.** A message that reads as a template gets treated as
> one. The personalised clause is the whole difference between a 5% and a 30% acceptance rate.

---

## The base template

> Hi [Name] — I'm [your name], [one-line who you are]. I saw you're working as [their role] at
> [company], which is exactly the kind of work I'm moving toward. I'd love to hear about your
> experience there. Would you be open to connecting?

**Filled in:**

> Hi Priya — I'm Sam, just finished a CS masters working on computer vision. I saw you're on the
> perception team at Nuro, which is exactly the kind of work I'm moving toward. I'd love to hear
> about your experience there. Would you be open to connecting?

**Why it works:** who you are, why *them* specifically, a low-cost ask, and no request for a job.

---

## Variant 1 — Shared school (highest response rate)

Lead with the school. If you have this, use it — nothing else converts as well.

> Hi [Name] — fellow [University] grad here. I'm finishing [program] and saw you've been at
> [company] as a [role] since graduating. Always good to see where [University] people end up.
> Would love to connect and hear how you found the transition.

---

## Variant 2 — Their work specifically

For someone who has published, spoken, or shipped something visible. Highest quality, most effort.

> Hi [Name] — I read your post on [specific thing] and it lined up with something I ran into
> building [your project]. I'm [one-line who you are] and I'm moving toward [role] work. Would
> like to connect and follow what your team is doing.

---

## Variant 3 — Shared community

Meetup, Discord, open-source project, conference, professional group.

> Hi [Name] — we're both in [community/group]. I'm [one-line who you are], currently focused on
> [area]. Saw you're at [company] doing [role] — that's the direction I'm heading. Would be great
> to connect.

---

## Variant 4 — After an event (near-100% acceptance)

Send within 24 hours. Do not let this one wait.

> Hi [Name] — great meeting you at [event] last night. Really enjoyed the conversation about
> [specific thing you actually discussed]. Would like to stay in touch.

---

## Variant 5 — After engaging with their content

For when you have commented on their posts a few times first. This is the warmest cold contact
there is.

> Hi [Name] — I've been following your posts on [topic] for a while and they've been genuinely
> useful. I'm [one-line who you are], working toward [role]. Would like to connect properly.

---

## Variant 6 — Same career transition

For someone who came from where you are now.

> Hi [Name] — I saw you moved from [their previous field/role] into [current role] at [company].
> I'm making a similar move from [yours] and would really value hearing how you approached it.
> Would you be open to connecting?

---

## Variant 7 — Recruiter at a target company

Different audience. Be direct — that is their job.

> Hi [Name] — I'm [one-line who you are], focused on [specialization]. I've been following
> [company] and I'm interested in [team/type of role]. Would like to connect and keep an eye on
> what opens up on your side.

---

## Variant 8 — After a funding announcement

> Hi [Name] — congratulations on the [Series X]. I work on [your specialization] and I've been
> following what [company] is doing with [specific thing]. Would like to connect and follow how
> the team grows from here.

---

## Rules

**Do**

- Personalise a specific clause — their team, their post, their path. One real detail is enough
- Say what you want: to hear about their experience. Nothing bigger
- Keep it under 300 characters
- Send Tuesday or Thursday, 10-11 AM their time, at a random minute
- Log it in `04_Trackers/outreach.csv` immediately

**Do not**

- Ask for a job, a referral, or "any openings" in the first message. This is the number one
  killer — it turns a friendly request into a transaction with a stranger
- Send a blank connection request. It does not count as contact
  ([Rule 1](../00_Start_Here/THE-RULES.md)) and it produces nothing
- Attach your resume to a connection request
- Say "I know you're busy" or "sorry to bother you" — it costs characters and lowers your status
- Use "I hope this message finds you well"
- Send the identical text to eight people at the same company. They talk to each other

---

## After they accept

**Do not immediately pitch.** Wait a day or two, then send one message:

> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. I'm currently [what you're doing] and looking toward [role]
> work. If you ever have 15 minutes, I'd really value hearing how you got into [their area] and
> what the team is like at [company] — completely understand if not.

That is the coffee chat ask. Notice it is still not a request for a job.

**If they do not reply:** engage with their content for a few weeks and try again with something
real to say. An accepted connection is a permanent, free channel — it has no expiry.
