# The Rules

> Nine non-negotiables. Everything else in this repo is guidance you can adapt. These are the
> things the crew agrees to hold each other to, which means breaking one is a thing your crew
> partner is *supposed* to raise with you.
>
> Each rule has a **why** and an **escape hatch**. Rules without escape hatches get broken
> quietly and then abandoned. These get broken loudly, on purpose, with a reason logged.

---

## 1. No application to a target company without contacting a human there first

**This is the rule.** If you only follow one, follow this one.

A cold application to a target company lands in a stack of 300–1000 with a human-read rate
somewhere around 5%. One message to one engineer on the team changes the odds more than every
resume edit you will make this year. The strongest hires in every mentor conversation behind
this repo were not applications — they were people who had already talked to someone inside.

**What counts as contact:** a LinkedIn connection request *with a note*, a cold email, a comment
they replied to, a conversation at an event, an alumni intro. A connection request with no note
does not count.

**What you do not have to do:** wait for a reply. Send the message, log it, then apply. The rule
is *reach out first*, not *get a response first* — because Rule 2 will not wait.

**Escape hatch:** non-target companies — volume applications to roles you would take but are not
chasing — are exempt. So is a posting that is closing in under 24 hours where you cannot find
a single human. Log the exception in `outreach.csv` with reason `no-contact-found` so the pattern
is visible if it becomes a habit.

---

## 2. Apply within 24 hours of the posting going live

First-24-hour applications show roughly **5x** the interview rate. The mechanism is unglamorous:
ATS queues are ordered by submission time, recruiters read the first batch, companies cap manual
review at a fixed number, and some recruiters carry fill-speed bonuses.

The brutal corollary: you can be the best candidate and be dropped because someone reached the
final round first and the manager decided they were good enough.

**Escape hatch:** an exceptional-fit role is worth applying to late — 22% of successful applicants
applied in the *last* 48 hours. Just never plan on being that person.

---

## 3. The sweep runs daily, not weekly

Rule 2 is only achievable if you see the posting on day zero. Fifteen minutes, every weekday,
before anything else. [The tool](../06_Tools/) exists to make it five.

**Escape hatch:** none on weekdays. Weekends are optional — postings go live Tue–Thu mostly.

---

## 4. Speed beats polish on the application itself

Every hour spent perfecting a resume is an hour the posting ages. Tailor the keywords, check the
format, send it. A good application today beats a perfect one on Thursday.

The exception is a genuine dream role with a referral already in hand — that one gets the full
treatment, because the referral has already bought you the human read that polish is trying to earn.

---

## 5. Outreach volume has a floor, and the floor is weekly

Set it in your own [goals file](../01_Crew/members/_TEMPLATE/GOALS.md) — a realistic starting
floor is **5–10 new contacts per week**, but pick a number you will actually hit and raise it
later. A floor you miss twice is the wrong floor; change it rather than ignoring it.

Networking is the highest-leverage activity and the one most likely to be silently dropped,
because it is uncomfortable and has no deadline. That is precisely why it gets a hard number.

---

## 6. Every contact gets logged, immediately

`04_Trackers/outreach.csv`, at send time — not at the end of the week when you have forgotten
who you messaged and when the follow-up is due. Untracked outreach means no follow-ups, and the
follow-up is where most replies actually come from.

---

## 7. Follow up. Twice. Then stop.

Roughly one week apart. Most people who eventually reply do not reply to the first message.

It is not annoying at two follow-ups. It is annoying at five. After the second, mark the contact
`cold` and move on — there is no shortage of people, and persistence past the point of welcome
costs you a contact permanently.

---

## 8. Post on LinkedIn at least once a week

Visibility is a months-long compounding game, not a campaign. Recruiters and engineers hiring
for a team look at people they recognise. Recognition takes repetition.

One post a week. Progress, a project, a thing you learned, a certification, a take on something
in your field. It does not have to be good. It has to be *there*.

---

## 9. The weekly check-in gets written and gets read

Both halves. Writing yours and not reading theirs makes it a diary, and the point is the crew.
Missing one is fine. Missing two in a row is the signal the protocol exists to catch — see
[the accountability protocol](../01_Crew/ACCOUNTABILITY-PROTOCOL.md).

---

## What to do when you break one

Log it and say so at the check-in. Not as penance — as data. A rule broken three weeks running
is either the wrong rule or a real obstacle, and both of those are things the crew should be
fixing rather than tolerating quietly.
